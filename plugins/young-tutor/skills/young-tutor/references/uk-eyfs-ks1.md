# UK school topics: EYFS and KS1

Use this file when the family lives in the UK and the topic is a school topic. EYFS and KS1 are the frameworks for England. The text below is a short summary. It is not the full statutory text.

## Year groups in England

The school year starts in September. The year group depends on the age of the child on 1 September.

| Age on 1 September | Year group | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Nursery | EYFS |
| 4 | Reception | EYFS |
| 5 | Year 1 | KS1 |
| 6 | Year 2 | KS1 |
| 7 | Year 3 | KS2 |

The age alone can give the wrong year group. Ask the parent for the year group.

## Other UK nations

EYFS and KS1 are for England. If the family lives in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland:

1. Tell the parent that the school uses a different curriculum:
   - Scotland: Curriculum for Excellence (Early level, then First level). P1 is the first year of school.
   - Wales: Curriculum for Wales (progression steps).
   - Northern Ireland: Foundation Stage (P1 and P2), then Key Stage 1 (P3 and P4).
2. Ask which programme the school uses. Many schools in these nations use the same phonics and maths programmes as schools in England.
3. Use EYFS and KS1 as a general guide for the ladder. Follow the programme of the school.

## EYFS: Early Learning Goals (end of Reception)

### Literacy: word reading

- Say a sound for each letter of the alphabet and at least 10 digraphs.
- Read words that agree with their phonic knowledge, by sound-blending.
- Read aloud simple sentences and books that agree with their phonic knowledge, with some common exception words.

### Literacy: writing

- Write letters that others can recognise. Most letters are formed correctly.
- Spell words: find the sounds in a word and write a letter or letters for each sound.
- Write simple phrases and sentences that others can read.

### Mathematics: number

- Understand numbers to 10 well, with the parts that make each number.
- Subitise up to 5 (see a small quantity and know the number without counting).
- Recall number bonds up to 5 automatically (with subtraction facts), and some number bonds to 10, with double facts.

### Mathematics: numerical patterns

- Count aloud past 20 and see the pattern of the counting system.
- Compare quantities up to 10: more than, fewer than, the same as.
- Find patterns in numbers up to 10: odd and even numbers, doubles, and how to share quantities equally.

## KS1 maths (summary)

### Year 1

- Count to and across 100, forwards and backwards, from any number.
- Read and write numbers to 100 in numerals. Count in 2s, 5s and 10s.
- Find one more and one less than a number.
- Number bonds and subtraction facts within 20.
- Add and subtract one-digit and two-digit numbers to 20, with zero.
- Find and name a half and a quarter of a shape, an object or a quantity.
- Compare lengths, heights, mass, capacity and time.
- Recognise coins and notes.
- Tell the time to the hour and half past the hour.
- Name common 2-D and 3-D shapes. Half, quarter and three-quarter turns.

### Year 2

- Count in steps of 2, 3 and 5 from 0, and in tens from any number.
- Place value in two-digit numbers (tens and ones). Compare and order numbers to 100 with <, > and =.
- Recall addition and subtraction facts to 20 fluently. Use them to find related facts to 100.
- Add and subtract with objects, pictures and mentally: a two-digit number and ones, a two-digit number and tens, two two-digit numbers, three one-digit numbers.
- Recall multiplication and division facts for the 2, 5 and 10 times tables. Odd and even numbers.
- Fractions: 1/3, 1/4, 2/4 and 3/4. Know that 2/4 is equal to 1/2.
- Money: pounds (£) and pence (p).
- Tell the time to 5 minutes, with quarter past and quarter to.
- Properties of 2-D and 3-D shapes: sides, edges, vertices and faces.
- Pictograms, tally charts, block diagrams and simple tables.

## KS1 English (summary)

### Year 1

- Reading: use phonics to decode words. Give the correct sound quickly for the graphemes of all 40+ phonemes. Read common exception words. Read words with the endings -s, -es, -ing, -ed, -er and -est.
- Writing: spell words with the 40+ phonemes. Name the letters of the alphabet in order. Form lower-case letters correctly. Use capital letters and full stops. Join words with "and".
- Phonics screening check: in June of Year 1. The check has 40 words. Some are real words and some are nonsense words ("alien words"). Children who do not meet the standard take the check again in Year 2.

### Year 2

- Reading: read words with two or more syllables and words with common suffixes. Read more common exception words. Read most words quickly, without sounding out.
- Writing: spell words with contractions (can't) and the possessive apostrophe (the girl's book). Use the suffixes -ment, -ness, -ful, -less and -ly. Use capital letters, full stops, question marks, exclamation marks and commas in lists. Join ideas with "when", "if", "that" and "because".

## Phonics

Schools in England teach reading with systematic synthetic phonics (SSP). Most schools use a programme from the Department for Education list of validated programmes. Examples of programmes that schools use:

- Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised
- Read Write Inc. Phonics
- Jolly Phonics
- Twinkl Phonics
- Essential Letters and Sounds
- Monster Phonics
- Sounds-Write

The list changes. If you are not sure, tell the parent to ask the school.

Programmes are different in these ways:

- The order of the sounds.
- The names of the stages. For example, "Phase 2" (Little Wandle) or "Set 1 sounds" (Read Write Inc.).
- The name for words that do not follow the phonics rules. For example, "tricky words" (Little Wandle) or "red words" (Read Write Inc.).
- The phrases for letter formation and for blending. For example, "Fred Talk" (Read Write Inc.).

Rules:

1. Ask which sounds the child knows. The school often sends home a sound mat, a sound chart or a reading book.
2. Follow the order of the school. Do not teach a sound before the school does, unless the parent asks.
3. Use the words of the school programme.
4. Use pure sounds. Read `phonics-pure-sounds.md`.

### General order of sounds

Many programmes use an order that is similar to Letters and Sounds (2007). Use this order only if you do not know the programme.

| Phase | Usual time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nursery | Listening, rhythm, rhyme, first sounds, oral blending (c-a-t → cat) |
| 2 | Reception, autumn | s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f ff l ll ss. Read and spell CVC words (sat, pin). First tricky words: the, to, I, no, go, into |
| 3 | Reception | j v w x y z zz qu, then ch sh th ng, then ai ee igh oa oo ar or ur ow oi ear air ure er. More tricky words: he, she, we, me, be, was, you, they, all, are, my, her |
| 4 | Reception, summer | Words with adjacent consonants (jump, stop, frog). No new sounds |
| 5 | Year 1 | New spellings for known sounds (ay ou ie ea oy ir ue aw wh ph ew oe au ey a-e e-e i-e o-e u-e). Other sounds for known spellings (the "ow" in cow and in snow) |
| 6 | Year 2 | Spelling rules, suffixes and past tense |

## Maths

Many schools use a "mastery" approach, with small steps. Examples of maths programmes: White Rose Maths, Maths — No Problem!, Power Maths and NCETM Mastering Number (Reception to Year 2).

Common tools and words:

- Ten frame, part-whole model (also "cherry model"), number line, hundred square, Numicon, Base 10 (Dienes).
- "Ones" and "tens" (not "units").
- "Number bonds", "subitise", "more than", "fewer than", "take away", "equals".

Use real objects first, then pictures, then numbers. Schools call this concrete, pictorial, abstract (CPA).
