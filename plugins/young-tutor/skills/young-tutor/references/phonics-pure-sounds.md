# Pure sounds: a guide for the parent

Use this file when you teach phonics. Give the parent the correct sound each time that a new sound comes in the lesson.

## The two rules

1. Say the sound, not the letter name. Say "sss", not "ess".
2. Do not add "uh" at the end of a sound. Say "mmm", not "muh". Say a quick "t", not "tuh".

Tell the parent why: the child blends the sounds into a word. Extra "uh" sounds make the word wrong. "Cuh-a-tuh" does not sound like "cat".

## How to write a sound for the parent

Write the sound between slashes. Then give a guide in quotes and a key word.

Example: /s/ (say "sss", like a snake). Key word: sun.

## Bouncy sounds

Say these sounds quickly. Do not add "uh".

| Sound | How to say it | Key word |
|---|---|---|
| /t/ | a quick "t" (whisper it) | tap |
| /p/ | a quick "p" (whisper it; feel a small puff of air) | pan |
| /k/ (c, k, ck) | a quick "k" (whisper it) | cat, kit, duck |
| /ch/ | a quick "ch", like a sneeze | chip |
| /b/ | a very short "b" | bat |
| /d/ | a very short "d" | dog |
| /g/ | a very short "g" | gap |
| /j/ | a very short "j" | jam |

## Sounds that need care

| Sound | How to say it | Key word |
|---|---|---|
| /h/ | a quiet breath out, like steam on a mirror. Not "huh" | hat |
| /w/ | round lips and a short "w". Not "wuh" | web |
| /y/ | a short "y", as at the start of "yes". Not "yuh" | yes |
| /x/ | two sounds, "k-s", as at the end of "box" | fox |
| /qu/ | two sounds, "k-w", as at the start of "queen" | queen |

## Digraphs and trigraphs

A digraph is two letters that make one sound. A trigraph is three letters that make one sound.

| Letters | Key word | Letters | Key word |
|---|---|---|---|
| ai | rain | ow | cow |
| ee | bee | oi | coin |
| igh | night | ear | hear |
| oa | boat | air | chair |
| oo (long) | zoo | ure | pure |
| oo (short) | book | er | hammer |
| ar | car | ch | chip |
| or | fork | sh | ship |
| ur | turn | th | thumb |

Note: "er" in "hammer" is a short, soft "uh" sound. This is the one sound where "uh" is correct.

## Blending and segmenting

- **Blending (for reading):** say each sound, then push the sounds together. /c/ /a/ /t/ → "cat". Start with oral blending: the parent says the sounds and the child says the word. Many schools call this "sound talk". Read Write Inc. calls it "Fred Talk".
- **Segmenting (for spelling):** the child says the word, then splits it into sounds. The child can count the sounds on their fingers.
- Use the words of the school programme.

## Tips for the parent

- Sounds change a little with accent. Use the sounds of your own accent. Pure sounds are more important than accent.
- Many phonics programmes have short videos for parents on their websites. Ask the school which video to watch.
